# Tone of Voice: Kidical Mass Belgium

> Derived from desk research: the current site, the Leticia interview, press coverage, the volunteer page, chapter pages (Mons, Namur, Liège), the manifesto, and the sponsor charter.

## Who Is Speaking

Kidical Mass Belgium is not an institution. It's a citizens' movement, run by parents, neighbours, cycling enthusiasts, and community builders who mostly have nothing to do with cycling professionally. The voice is warm, energetic, inclusive, and occasionally a little breathless with enthusiasm. It's the voice of someone who genuinely believes in what they're doing and wants you to come along.

Leticia: "Kidical Mass is a citizens' movement of people who actually have nothing to do with cycling itself, but who build a beautiful community."

That is the voice. Community-first. Joy-first. Cycling is the vehicle, not the point.

---

## The Four Voice Qualities

### 1. Joyful (not frivolous)

Events are festive: music, coloured vests, cargo bikes with sound systems, kids on decorated bikes. The voice reflects that energy. But underneath the celebration is a real political mission: safer streets, the right of children to move freely. The joy is genuine, and so is the conviction.

- Use vivid, sensory language: music, colour, pace, laughter, neighbourhood
- Don't flatten the celebration into corporate cheerfulness
- Don't let the advocacy tip into severity; keep the invitation open

✓ "Nous pédalons au rythme des enfants, avec de la musique en cours de route."
✗ "Our evidence-based approach to child mobility promotes sustainable urban transport outcomes."

### 2. Warm and inclusive (genuinely, not performatively)

Anyone who reads it should feel they belong before they've done anything. Address barriers explicitly and dissolve them.

- Address "you" directly and warmly, not "participants" or "members"
- Name the barriers and dissolve them: no bike? no problem. Never cycled on the road? that's fine.
- Avoid insider language without explanation
- Keep age ranges, distances, and duration concrete: they are the reassurance

✓ "Pour de nombreux parents, c'est souvent la première fois qu'ils utilisent un vélo sur la route."
✗ "Experienced and novice cyclists are equally welcome at our events."

### 3. Local and grounded (not generic)

The most vivid writing is on chapter pages: Mons names its team (Violette, Babas, Sébastien et Thibault), its partners, its meeting point (Théâtre le Manège). This specificity is part of the brand. Kidical Mass doesn't happen in a generic city. It happens in your municipality, organised by your neighbours.

- Always name the place: not "Brussels" but "Schaerbeek" or "Forest/Vorst"
- Use local landmarks as meeting points, not generic coordinates
- Name the local team where possible
- Connect to local events, seasons, neighbourhood life

✓ "Départ du kiosque Woodpecker dans le Bois de la Cambre."
✗ "Meet at the designated starting location."

### 4. Committed (not preachy)

Kidical Mass has a clear political position: children have the right to safe streets. But the rides are framed as celebration first, demonstration second. They position as citizens, not activists.

- State the position clearly when context calls for it (manifesto, press, advocacy pages)
- On event pages and family-facing content, lead with experience not argument
- Never moralise about car culture; show the alternative instead
- Trust the ride itself to make the point

✓ "En étant visible dans les rues, la Kidical Mass prône de façon ludique une ville plus adaptée aux enfants."
✗ "Car dependency is destroying our children's right to public space. Join us to fight back."

## Tone by Context

| Context | Register |
|---|---|
| Event pages, homepage | Warm, inviting, concrete: answer the practical questions joyfully |
| Volunteer / Contribute | Energetic, honest about what's involved, make it feel doable |
| Chapter pages | Local, personal, specific: this is your neighbourhood |
| Mission / About | Confident, grounded, community-first, not institutional |
| Partners / Sponsors | A notch more serious, still human, not corporate |
| Press | Clear, factual, with warmth, not a press release template |

## The Bilingual Dimension

- **Translate meaning, not words.** "Nos kets" (Brussels slang for kids) doesn't survive literal translation. Find the NL equivalent that carries the same affection.
- **Each language version stands alone.** A French reader shouldn't feel they're reading a translation.
- **Chapter language follows the chapter.** Wallonia = FR. Flanders = NL. Brussels = both. Don't force bilingualism at chapter level.
- **Bilingual URL slugs are not a solution.** `/nos-revendications-onze-aanbevelingen` is a symptom of the old approach, not a feature to preserve.

## Reference Phrases (Voice at Its Best)

- "Viens rouler avec nous !" Simple, direct, warm. No friction.
- "Nous pédalons au rythme des enfants": specific, reassuring physical reality.
- "Une masse de kets contents": playful, local, affectionate.
- "Make streets for kids happen": action-oriented, collective, optimistic. Works across languages.
- "100% vélo, 4 yummy meetups per jaar, massa's nieuwe fietsbuddy's": mixes formality and informality confidently.
- "Kidical Mass, c'est ta fête de quartier": neighbourhood party framing. Lowers the stakes, raises the appeal.

## What to Avoid

- **Em-dashes (—) in copy.** Don't use the em-dash as a connector. It's one of the clearest tells of AI-generated text, and it flattens the rhythm of a sentence. Reach for a full stop (two thoughts), a comma (a short aside), or a colon (introducing something) instead. Hyphens inside compound words (*roze-hesje*, *Woom-fietsen*) are fine; it's the free-standing dash that's banned. This applies to all interface copy, in every language.
- **Corporate positivity.** "We are committed to inclusive mobility solutions": accurate but dead.
- **Jargon without explanation.** "Gilets roses", "porteurs locaux", "espacefunding": introduce them to newcomers.
- **Bilingual stacking.** Two monolingual pages stapled together is not bilingualism.
- **Manufactured urgency.** "We need you NOW" undermines the warmth.
- **Over-explaining the politics.** Trust the reader to connect the dots.

## The One-Line Test

Before publishing anything: **does this sound like someone who loves cycling with kids in their neighbourhood and wants you to come along?**

If yes: publish.
If it sounds like a policy paper, a corporate newsletter, or a guilt trip: rewrite.
