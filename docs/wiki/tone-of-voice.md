## Who Is Speaking

Kidical Mass Belgium is not an institution. It's a citizens' movement — run by parents, neighbours, cycling enthusiasts, and community builders who mostly have nothing to do with cycling professionally. The voice that comes through in the existing content is warm, energetic, inclusive, and occasionally a little breathless with enthusiasm. It's the voice of someone who genuinely believes in what they're doing and wants you to come along.

Founder Leticia put it best in her own words: *"Kidical Mass is a citizens' movement of people who actually have nothing to do with cycling itself, but who build a beautiful community."*

That is the voice. Community-first. Joy-first. Cycling is the vehicle, not the point.

## The Statement

The north star — one sentence that captures *how* and *why* Kidical Mass sounds the way it does. Check every piece of copy against it.

> **Kidical Mass sounds like an enthusiastic neighbour: warm, joyful and concrete, inviting every family — bike or no bike — to help make streets for kids happen.**

Renderings in the brand languages (each stands alone, not a translation of the other):

- **FR** — *« Kidical Mass parle comme un·e voisin·e enthousiaste : chaleureux, joyeux et concret, on invite chaque famille — avec ou sans vélo — à faire de la rue un espace pour les enfants. »*

## The Voice Qualities

### 1. Joyful — but not frivolous

Kidical Mass events are festive. There's music, coloured vests, cargo bikes with sound systems, kids on decorated bikes, giant cakes at the finish line. The voice reflects that energy — it should make you want to show up.

But underneath the celebration is a real political mission: safer streets, better infrastructure, the right of children to move freely. The joy is genuine, and so is the conviction. Neither undermines the other.

**In practice:**
- Use vivid, sensory language: music, colour, pace, laughter, neighbourhood
- Don't flatten the celebration into corporate cheerfulness
- Don't let the advocacy tip into severity — keep the invitation open

**Good:** *"Nous pédalons au rythme des enfants, avec de la musique en cours de route."*
**Avoid:** *"Our evidence-based approach to child mobility promotes sustainable urban transport outcomes."*

---

### 2. Warm and inclusive — genuinely, not performatively

The audience is explicitly broad: families with children 3–12, beginners, people without bikes, people new to cycling, people from every neighbourhood background. The Leticia interview names Nadia and Saïda — two women who only learned to cycle a few years ago — as local ride organisers. That's the inclusion model in action.

The voice should feel like an open door, not a membership card. Anyone who reads it should feel they belong before they've done anything.

**In practice:**
- Address "you" directly and warmly — not "participants" or "members"
- Name the barriers explicitly and dissolve them: *no bike? no problem. Never cycled on the road? that's fine.*
- Avoid insider language: "gilets roses" is charming in context, explain it for newcomers
- Keep age ranges, distances, and duration concrete — they are the reassurance

**Good:** *"Pour de nombreux parents, c'est souvent la première fois qu'ils utilisent un vélo sur la route."*
**Avoid:** *"Experienced and novice cyclists are equally welcome at our events."*

---

### 3. Local and grounded — not generic

The current site's most vivid writing is on the chapter pages: Mons names its team (Violette, Babas, Sébastien et Thibault), its local partners, its history, its meeting point (Théâtre le Manège, Rue des Trois Boudins). Namur names its streets and its press coverage. Liège describes the specific obstacles course and the BMX show waiting at the finish.

This specificity is part of the brand. Kidical Mass doesn't happen in a generic city — it happens *in your municipality*, on routes through your parks and past your schools, organised by your neighbours.

**In practice:**
- Always name the place: not "Brussels" but "Schaerbeek" or "Forest/Vorst"
- Use local landmarks as meeting points, not generic coordinates
- Name the local team where possible — people, not just an organisation
- Connect to local events, seasons, neighbourhood life

**Good:** *"Départ du kiosque Woodpecker dans le Bois de la Cambre."*
**Avoid:** *"Meet at the designated starting location."*

---

### 4. Committed — but not preachy

Kidical Mass has a clear political position: children have the right to safe streets. The manifesto, the revendications page, and the press coverage all articulate this confidently. But the existing communication is careful not to lecture — the rides are framed as celebration first, demonstration second.

Leticia described this deliberately: they position as citizens, not activists. In difficult political times (her words, re: Good Move in Brussels), they stay joyful and constructive rather than combative.

**In practice:**
- State the position clearly when the context calls for it (manifesto, press releases, revendications page)
- On event pages and family-facing content, lead with experience not argument
- Never moralise about car culture — show the alternative instead
- Trust the ride itself to make the point

**Good:** *"En étant visible dans les rues, la Kidical Mass prône de façon ludique une ville plus adaptée aux enfants."*
**Avoid:** *"Car dependency is destroying our children's right to public space. Join us to fight back."*

---

### 5. Concrete — facts are the reassurance

For a first-time family, the warmth only lands if the practical questions are answered. Ages, distances, duration, pace, meeting point, what to bring — these aren't logistics buried at the bottom, they *are* the welcome. Specifics lower the threshold; abstractions raise it. (This trait runs through the *local* and *inclusive* qualities above — it's worth naming on its own because it's the easiest one to forget under deadline.)

**In practice:**
- Lead with the numbers: age range, distance in km, duration, start time
- A named meeting point beats coordinates; "casque conseillé" beats "safety equipment recommended"
- When in doubt, picture the hesitant parent reading on their phone the night before

**Good:** *"Une boucle de 4 km au rythme des enfants, environ une heure, départ 14h. Casque conseillé, bonne humeur obligatoire."*
**Avoid:** *"A family-friendly ride suitable for all ages and abilities."*

## Tone by Context

| Context | Register |
|---|---|
| Event pages, homepage | Warm, inviting, concrete — answer the practical questions joyfully |
| Volunteer / Contribute | Energetic, honest about what's involved, make it feel doable |
| Chapter pages | Local, personal, specific — this is your neighbourhood |
| Mission / About | Confident, grounded, community-first — not institutional |
| Partners / Sponsors | A notch more serious, still human — not corporate |
| Press | Clear, factual, with warmth — not a press release template |

## The Bilingual Dimension

Kidical Mass publishes in both French and Dutch. The voice qualities above apply equally in both languages, but there are a few practical principles:

- **Translate meaning, not words.** The warmth of *"nos kets"* (Brussels slang for kids) doesn't survive literal translation — find the NL equivalent that carries the same affection.
- **Each language version stands alone.** A French reader shouldn't feel they're reading a translation. Same for Dutch.
- **Chapter language follows the chapter.** Wallonia chapters are FR. Flemish chapters are NL. Brussels is both. Don't force bilingualism at chapter level — it dilutes rather than serves.
- **Bilingual slugs are not a solution.** URL slugs like `/nos-revendications-onze-aanbevelingen` are a symptom of the current structural problem, not a feature to preserve.

## Reference Phrases

These are drawn directly from the existing content. They represent the voice at its best.

- *"Viens rouler avec nous !"* — Simple, direct, warm invitation. No friction.
- *"Nous pédalons au rythme des enfants"* — Grounds the experience in a specific, reassuring physical reality.
- *"Une masse de kets contents"* — Playful, local, affectionate. Lets the joy be the argument.
- *"Make streets for kids happen"* — The English tagline. Action-oriented, collective, optimistic. Works across languages.
- *"100% vélo, 4 yummy meetups per jaar, massa's nieuwe fietsbuddy's"* — The NL volunteer pitch mixes formality and informality confidently. Not trying too hard.
- *"Kidical Mass, c'est ta fête de quartier"* — Neighbourhood party framing. Lowers the stakes, raises the appeal.

## Terminology & Word List

A consistent vocabulary strengthens identity. Prefer the left column; avoid the right. For *definitions* of event terms (fietsparade vs. stoet, etc.) see the [glossary](glossary.md) — this table is about the *register* choice between near-synonyms.

| Prefer | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| citizens' movement / mouvement citoyen / burgerbeweging | organisation, NGO, association | They self-identify as citizens, not an institution |
| family, families / familles / gezinnen | participants, attendees, members | People, not a headcount |
| neighbourhood / quartier / buurt | catchment area, zone, location | Grounds the event in lived place |
| join us, come ride / viens rouler / fiets mee | register, sign up, enrol | An invitation, not a transaction |
| make streets for kids happen | promote sustainable mobility solutions | The brand's own action-line beats jargon |
| safe streets / rues sûres / veilige straten | traffic-calming measures, modal shift | Plain language the audience uses |
| volunteers in pink / gilet rose / roze hesje *(explained)* | gilets roses *(cold, unexplained)* | Charming once introduced, opaque before |
| kids / enfants / kinderen, kets *(BXL, informal)* | minors, juveniles | Affection over administration |

*Note:* *"kets"* (Brussels slang for kids) is a deliberate warmth marker — use it where the audience is Brussels and the register is informal, not in press or partner copy.

## What to Avoid

- **Corporate positivity.** Phrases like "we are committed to inclusive mobility solutions" are accurate but dead. The voice is human, not institutional.
- **Jargon without explanation.** Terms like "gilets roses", "porteurs locaux", "spacefunding" are meaningful inside the community. Introduce them to newcomers.
- **Bilingual stacking as an afterthought.** Pasting the NL version below the FR (or vice versa) with a horizontal line between them is not bilingualism — it's two monolingual pages stapled together. Each language should feel like a first draft, not a translation.
- **Urgency for its own sake.** The movement is growing steadily and healthily. Manufactured emergency language ("we need you NOW") undermines the warmth.
- **Over-explaining the politics.** The rides speak for themselves. Trust the reader to make the connection between a hundred children cycling safely through their neighbourhood and the kind of city they want.

## The One-Line Test

Before publishing anything: **does this sound like someone who loves cycling with kids in their neighbourhood and wants you to come along?**

If yes: publish.
If it sounds like a policy paper, a corporate newsletter, or a guilt trip: rewrite.

## Register dial

One axis — **joyful & warm ←————→ serious & factual** — with two hard stops it must never cross: **corporate** and **militant / preachy** (and never hardcore-cyclist). Where each context sits (the dial form of *Tone by Context* above):

```
joyful/warm ●──────────────────────────────────● serious/factual
  home,        help out,    mission /    vision/manifesto,
  events,      chapters     about        press releases,
  getting started                        partners · press
```

- **Family-facing** (home, events, getting started, chapters): far warm end — answer practical questions joyfully.
- **Help out:** warm, but honest about the commitment.
- **Mission / About:** warm-confident, community-first.
- **Vision / manifesto / press releases:** state the position clearly; more serious, still human.
- **Partners / sponsors / press:** a notch more factual — never corporate.

## Pre-ship checklist

The operational gate before publishing any copy:

- [ ] Passes the one-line test (above).
- [ ] Register matches the context on the dial.
- [ ] Names the place / people / landmarks where relevant (local, not generic).
- [ ] Barriers named and dissolved (*no bike? no problem*); age / distance / duration concrete.
- [ ] Speaks to "you", not "participants" / "members".
- [ ] Insider terms (roze hesje / gilet rose, spacefunding) introduced for newcomers.
- [ ] No corporate positivity, manufactured urgency, or moralising.
- [ ] Each language reads as an original, not a translation.
- [ ] Correct glossary terms — *fietsparade / parade*, not *stoet / rit* ([glossary](glossary.md)).
